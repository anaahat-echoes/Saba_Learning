# Saba English — Phonics Tutoring Context

## The project

An informal one-on-one phonics tutoring arrangement. Weekly classes. The curriculum creator / tutor is a software professional (not an educator) who loves teaching young children. Sessions are conducted virtually (video call). Twinkl subscription is available for worksheets.

The goal is to help a young child build strong English reading foundations. This is a long-term project tracked in a private GitHub repo.

---

## Student profile

- Young Kashmiri girl, aged 6
- English is her second language; it is not spoken at home. Parents understand it but not fluent on advanced pronunciation and vocabulary.
- School is running fast-paced, teaching phonics in alphabetical order (a, b, c, d — two letters a day). We will ignore this methodology.

| Vowel | Status |
|-------|--------|
| A (short) | Comfortable — solid foundation |

| I (short) | Struggling more — likely because school has not set the right foundations |

**Critical gap identified: blending**

She has not been taught blending properly. She is getting by, but the foundation is weak for future reading progress. This must be treated as a parallel priority alongside vowel work.

## Learning plan — 4 phases

### Phase 1 — Consolidate A (1–2 sessions)

Use the comfortable A sound as the vehicle to properly teach blending for the first time.

- **Word set:** cat, hat, map, pan, bat, ran, sad, fan
- **Activities:** picture-word match, rhyme games, Elkonin box tapping
- **Gate 1:** Reads 6/8 CVC-A words independently without prompting

### Phase 2 — Introduce E (2–3 sessions)

Key challenge: E sounds similar to A in some contexts. Always contrast A vs E side by side.

- **Word set:** bed, red, hen, pet, web, ten, jet, peg
- **Activities:** sound sorting (A pile vs E pile), picture cards
- **Gate 2:** Blends 5 CVC-E words aloud and correctly sorts A vs E picture cards

### Phase 3 — Master I (2–3 sessions)

Hardest vowel. The short-I sound (/ɪ/) is likely unfamiliar from her home language.

- **Word set:** big, pig, sit, win, bin, lid, tip, him
- **Activities:** three-vowel sort (A/E/I), minimal pairs (bit/bat/bet)
- **Gate 3:** Sorts picture cards into A, E, I groups with 80%+ accuracy

### Phase 4 — Reading fluency

Mix all three vowels in decodable sentences.

- Example: "The big cat sat on a red mat."
- Move to simple decodable books (Usborne phonics readers, Bob Books — scan and present in class).

---

## Blending thread (runs through ALL phases)

This is the most important fix right now.

- **Method:** Tap each sound on fingers (c-a-t = 3 taps), then sweep fingers together saying the whole word
- **Rule:** No letter names — only sounds
- **Technique name:** Elkonin boxes / "say it slow, say it fast"
- **Never** jump in immediately on errors — give 3–5 seconds for self-correction first

---

## Pedagogy notes

Session structure: **5 min oral warm-up → 15 min worksheet/activity → 5 min end on a win**

- "I do, we do, you do" — model the word first, read together, then she reads alone
- End every session on something she can do confidently — last emotional memory shapes return motivation
- Spaced repetition: start each session with a 5-minute review of last session's words
- Don't correct immediately — wait 3–5 seconds for self-correction before stepping in
- Track a "known words" list — visible progress motivates young learners

## Resources

### Worksheets (Twinkl subscription available)

Suggested searches:
- `CVC word sort a e i` — picture sorting mats
- `Elkonin boxes phonics` — blending practice
- `Phonics minimal pairs` — for E vs I contrast

**Good worksheet criteria:**
- Shows picture + asks child to identify/circle the vowel (not just trace letters)
- Includes "odd one out" tasks
- Has a blending prompt, not just isolated letters
