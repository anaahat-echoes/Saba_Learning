# Saba English — Teaching Strategy

## Context

One-on-one phonics tutoring for a 6-year-old Kashmiri girl. English is her second language; not spoken at home. Sessions are weekly, conducted virtually (video call). Tutor is a software professional, not a trained educator.

School is teaching phonics alphabetically at speed (two letters/day). We are **not following that methodology** — we are building proper foundations first.

---

## Core teaching philosophy

1. **Blending before everything.** A child who can blend can decode any word they are taught. Blending is the lever.
2. **Confidence is a prerequisite for learning.** End every session on a win. The last emotional memory shapes whether she wants to come back.
3. **No letter names — only sounds.** Letter names ("aych", "doubleyou") actively confuse early readers. Always say the phoneme.
4. **Wait before correcting.** Give 3–5 seconds for self-correction. Jumping in immediately trains her to wait for you instead of thinking.
5. **Spaced repetition.** Every session begins with a 5-minute review of last session's words.
6. **Contrast is how E and I get learned.** Never teach a new vowel in isolation — always pair it against the previous one.

---

## Session structure (25 min)

| Segment | Time | Purpose |
|---------|------|---------|
| Warm-up | 0–5 min | Oral review of last session's words; short video clip |
| Core activity | 5–20 min | New teaching + worksheet |
| End on a win | 20–25 min | One word or task she can do alone and feel proud of |

**Teaching sequence within core activity:** I do → We do → You do
- Model the word/task yourself first
- Do it together
- She does it alone

---

## Blending method (Elkonin / finger-tap)

The single most important technique in this curriculum.

1. Say each phoneme while tapping a finger: /c/ (tap) — /a/ (tap) — /t/ (tap)
2. Sweep all fingers together while saying the whole word: "cat"
3. Name: "say it slow, say it fast"
4. Rule: no letter names, only sounds
5. Never skip this step, even for words she has already seen

## The four phases

### Phase 1 — Consolidate short A + teach blending (May 2026)

**Goal:** Use the familiar A sound as the vehicle to introduce proper blending for the first time.

| Item | Detail |
|------|--------|
| Word set | cat, hat, map, pan, bat, ran, sad, fan |
| Key activity | Word ladders (-at, -an family), picture-word match |
| Technique introduced | Finger-tap blending |
| **Gate 1** | Reads 6/8 CVC-A words independently without prompting |

### Phase 2 — Introduce short E (June 2026)

**Goal:** Introduce E while always contrasting it with A. The A/E distinction is the hardest thing at this stage.

| Item | Detail |
|------|--------|
| Word set | bed, red, hen, pet, web, ten, jet, peg |
| Key activity | Sound sorting (A pile vs E pile), picture cards |
| Critical rule | Never teach E alone — always A vs E side by side |
| **Gate 2** | Blends 5 CVC-E words aloud and correctly sorts A vs E picture cards |

### Phase 3 — Master short I (July 2026)

**Goal:** Crack the hardest vowel. /ɪ/ is likely not a sound in her home language.

| Item | Detail |
|------|--------|
| Word set | big, pig, sit, win, bin, lid, tip, him |
| Key activity | Three-vowel sort (A/E/I), minimal pairs (bit/bat/bet) |
| Critical rule | Use minimal pairs — bit vs bat vs bet — to make the contrast physical |
| **Gate 3** | Sorts picture cards into A, E, I groups with 80%+ accuracy |

### Phase 4 — Reading fluency (August 2026)

**Goal:** Mix all three vowels in decodable sentences and move to simple books.

| Item | Detail |
|------|--------|
| Example sentence | "The big cat sat on a red mat." |
| Books | Usborne phonics readers, Bob Books (scan pages to present in class) |
| **Gate 4** | Reads a 2–3 sentence decodable passage aloud with no more than 1 prompted stop |
